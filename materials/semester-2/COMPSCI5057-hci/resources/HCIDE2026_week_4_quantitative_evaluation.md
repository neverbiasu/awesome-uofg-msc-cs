M E A S U R E M E N T S C A L E S Nominal / categorical • Labels or names • Classifies data, but can’t rank them • These could be numbers, but can’t do computations with them, e.g. randomly generated IDs Ordinal • Can put the values in a ranking, but not equally spaced • e.g. ordered list of favourite films; slow vs medium vs fast • Can do > or < comparisons, but it is not valid to calculate means


<!-- Page 28 -->

M E A S U R E M E N T S C A L E S Interval • Equal distances between adjacent values, but lacking a ‘true’, absolute zero • Can compute mean • e.g. Celsius scale: you can take mean value of week’s temperature, but the absence of a true zero means proportions may not make sense: 20 ° C is not “twice as hot” as 10 ° • Likert scales • Sometimes treated as Ordinal or Interval. Important to know which if you want to compute means. Treating as Interval is OK if options are equally spaced and centred at neutral value


<!-- Page 29 -->

M E A S U R E M E N T S C A L E S Ratio • Do have absolute zero – cannot have negative values • Support many calculations • add, divide, mean, standard deviation… • e.g. time, distance, counts of events

H Y P O T H E S I S T E S T I N G You run a study, manipulate your IV, measure your DVs, and now want to prove that the effect you are trying to elicit exists! • e.g. using a DVORAK keyboard with 2 months of training leads to improved typing speed compared to QWERTY keyboards. • You measure 30 people: 15 w/ QWERTY , 15 w/ DVORAK . Looking at their means, the typing speed is higher for DOVRAK users by about 18 words per minute Given this data, how do we know that the difference didn’t occur by chance? • Maybe if you did the condition again, you would have a smaller difference? • Definite proof is often impossible: we look to statistics to see if we have sufficient evidence to see if our hypothesis is true. This is called Hypothesis Testing


<!-- Page 38 -->

H Y P O T H E S I S T E S T I N G To realise a statistical hypothesis test, we first define our hypothesis and the Null hypothesis : the opposite hypothesis where there is no effect • H1 (Alternative Hypothesis – what we really are trying to prove): DVORAK keyboard users have faster typing speed than QWERTY keyboard users • H0 (Null Hypothesis): There is no difference in typing speed between DVORAK and QWERTY keyboard users Statistical Tests allow us to to estimate how likely is it for H0 is false Hypothesis tests attempt to reject the Null hypothesis – if we reject it, then our original hypothesis is true (or at least statistically likely)
