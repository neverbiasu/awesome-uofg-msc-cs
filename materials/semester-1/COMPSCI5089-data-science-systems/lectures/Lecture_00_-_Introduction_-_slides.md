## Is data all you need?
Data science is a highly interdisciplinary activity concerned with gaining insights from often large and messy data which you sometimes have to collect, store and curate yourself. It is the application of data-centric, computer science, and inferential thinking to understand the world (science) and solve problems (engineering).

## Example of questions we might try to answer with data science
- How much exercise did you get in 2020?
- Is the use of the COMPAS algorithm for prison sentencing in the US fair?
- How will Brexit influence the UK economy?
- What should we eat to maximize our live expectancy?
- Who should get a COVID-19 vaccine first?
- Has the world gotten better or worse over the last 10 years (on average)?
- Will there be a forth wave of COVID19 in the UK?

## About this course
**Focus of this course**
- "How to represent data as numbers?" --- Vector spaces.
- "How to manipulate and transform data?" --- Computational linear algebra (e.g. PCA/SVD).
- "How to answer questions with data?" --- Numerical optimisation.
- "How to represent and reason with uncertainty?" --- Probability & the basics and inference.
- "How to store and access data?" --- Data storage

**Other skills**
- Numpy (in detail) and Pandas (self-study / recommended material)
- Visualization
- Critical analysis and reflection
- Practical with a real-world data science case

**The course is not:** Machine learning, artificial intelligence, a "tools course" teaching you specific data science systems.

## Practicalities
- **Credits and workload:** The course is a 15-credit course which amounts to about 150 hours of work.
- **Lectures (Thursdays):** In-person (in JMS building). Recordings of the live lecture will typically be provided on Moodle as Zoom/MS Streams
- **Lab sessions (Fridays):**
  - In Boyd Orr 1028
  - Prepare labs in advance to the lab session!
  - Peer support: Please be active in the MS Team groups and answer each other questions etc.
- **Assessment**
  - Labs (4 labs, each worth 8% of the final grade, recommended effort is 12 hours each) - deadlines posted on Moodle.
    - Labs are typically due 2/3 weeks after hand-out (see Moodle / LTC system for details).
  - Weekly quizzes (10 quizzes, 6% in total, top 70% counts, i.e. you can drop two quizzes and still get top mark)
  - Exam, written 2-hours in December.
- **Material:** The lecture notes (e.g. interactive notebooks, slides) with references to additional/supporting material. - Explore on your own! There are many excellent sources of information on the internet and we strongly encourage you to read outside the predefined curriculum.
- **A note on marking and effort:** The suggested 12 hours for each assessed lab is the time we expect you to spend on the lab (effective work hours) to get the grade you would normally get in a similar course. An A1 (top grade) student should spend 12h getting an A1, a B2 (average) student should spend 12h getting a B2, etc.
- **A note on plagiarism:** Don't do it! We automatically check all submissions against each other - and we are good at finding patterns. If you find yourself in a situation where copying other people's work is the only option then reach out to the teaching staff on the course and we can help/guide you.
- **A heads up:** We will be monitoring and analysing the progress throughout the 11 weeks - we are data scientists after all! We might make changes to the schedule as we go along based on your feedback and our observations.

## Feedback on your work
You will receive feedback on your work in a variety of ways in this module:
- The lab exercises are automarked based on running a range of tests on your code. The majority of those tests are visible: they will show you immediately if you've made a mistake.
- Additional hidden tests will be run on your work and the results (along with the mark) will be returned to you between 1 and 2 weeks after submission.
- One Lecturer and two Teaching Assistants will attend the weekly lab sessions and will be available to answer questions and provide advice on your work - **make the best use of this time!**
- The MSTeams channel is provided for discussions and questions about the course content.

## Student feedback
- We monitor student feedback on the course in a variety of ways throughout the year:
- You will be asked to fill module feedback surveys twice in the term: at midpoint and towards the end. Please, complete them - they are an essential tool for us to monitor and improve the delivery of the course (and as you'll learn in this module, small samples are not great!).
- You can raise issues via the student representatives for discussion in the Staff-Student Liaison Committees.
- Don't hesitate to talk to us, during lectures, labs or via MSTeams.
- **We value your opinion!** We cannot change *what* you have to learn in this term (you'll need it in other modules next term!), but we are always looking to improve *how* we teach it.
