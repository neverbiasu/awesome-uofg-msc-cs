Rank of a matrix  The   rank   of a matrix is equal to the number of non-zero singular values. If the number of non-zero singular values is equal to the size of the matrix, then the matrix is   full rank . A full rank matrix has a non-zero determinant and will be invertible. The rank tells us how many dimensions the parallelotope that the transform represents will have. If a matrix does not have full rank, it is   singular   (non-invertible) and has   deficient rank . If the number of non-zero singular values is much less than the size of the matrix, the matrix is   low rank . For example, a 4 x 4 matrix with rank 2 will take vectors in   and output vectors in a subspace (a plane) of   . The orientation of that plane will be given by the first two eigenvectors of the matrix.
