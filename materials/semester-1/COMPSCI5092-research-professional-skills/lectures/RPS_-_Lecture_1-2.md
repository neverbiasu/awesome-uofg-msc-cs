Confidential  Information
Designed to prevent someone revealing information they have received in  confidence  Requires an understanding (an “obligation of confidence”) that the information  was given in confidence  It is not required to be written down as explicitly being confidential  The law of confidential information
●   A useful tool for protecting commercially sensitive material such as trade  secrets which cannot be fully protected by intellectual property rights  ●   Based around the principle a person who has received information in  confidence should not use or disclose the information without permission  ●   Unauthorised use or disclosure of such information may give rise to an  action for breach of confidence  The law of confidential information
Imposed by a contract   -   e.g. NDA (non - disclosure agreements)  Implied by circumstances of discourse   -   “ determined on the basis of whether a  reasonable man standing in the shoes of the recipient of the information would  have realised that the information was being given to him in confidence ”  Implied because of a special relationship between the parties   -   e.g.  employee - employer  How can information be given in confidence
Trade secrets are a particular type of confidential information  ●   These are novel techniques used to develop particularly unique products  ●   An important but invisible part of a company’s intellectual property  ●   Must be protected to stop people just sharing them with their competitors  (e.g. disgruntled employees wanting a new job)  A quick note on trade secrets
