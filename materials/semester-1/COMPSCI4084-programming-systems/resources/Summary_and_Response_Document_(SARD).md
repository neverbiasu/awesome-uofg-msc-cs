Thank you for sharing this\. 

Previous years’ exam feedback also noted the duration of the Lab exam, and students said they did not have enough time to complete it\. In response to this feedback, this year the exam takes place in December rather than in week 6, as in previous years, giving students more time to prepare\. 

ProgSD is a course with a practice focus\. This year, we included a practical assessment that mirrors the Python section of the Lab exam\.

The exam builds on lab work and a clear understanding of programming concepts through practical tasks\. 

Typically, if a student has completed all the practical tasks, including attempting to replicate the codes in the PowerPoint slides and past papers, the exam would be easier, as it is based on these tasks\. In some cases, students are also given crucial exam tips that could help them reinforce their revision of the practical sections of the material covered by those tips\. The exam is open\-book, so students can easily refer to their solutions to complete their programming questions more quickly\. They would copy their code and make a few modifications \(provided they understand the programming concepts in the first place\) rather than spend time searching online \(AI search engine or Chat are not allowed\)\. This is what most students did in their practical assessment\. 

However, if students do not take the time to complete the practical tasks, they will face more difficulty meeting the exam time limit\. For example, they would be typing most of the code for the first time and therefore spending more time debugging\.
