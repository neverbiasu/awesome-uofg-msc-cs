What is classification in machine learning?	A supervised learning task to predict a discrete/categorical label.
How does classification differ from regression?	Classification predicts discrete labels; regression predicts continuous values.
What are the four main components of a classification system?	Labeled training data, features, model, evaluation metrics.
What is the role of "features" in a classification system?	Numerical representation of raw data for the model.
What is "labeled training data" in classification?	Dataset with examples `x` and corresponding correct labels `y`.
What is the "model" component in classification?	Algorithm learning relationship between features and labels.
What are "evaluation metrics" used for in classification?	Measure the model's performance (e.g., accuracy).
What is a "feature vector"?	A numerical representation of data's measurable properties.
What is the Bag-of-Words (BoW) model used for?	Representing text data as a vector of word counts/frequencies.
What is the "curse of dimensionality"?	Problems arising from using too many features (high dimensionality).
What is the goal of dimensionality reduction?	Reduce features while retaining important information.
What is PCA (Principal Component Analysis)?	A popular technique for dimensionality reduction.
How does PCA work?	Identifies directions (principal components) capturing maximum variance.
What does the first principal component represent in PCA?	Direction of maximum data spread.
What is the principle of Occam's Razor in model building?	Seek simpler models that explain data well.
Why is feature engineering considered key in traditional machine learning?	Greatest impact on model performance.
What type of learning algorithm is k-NN?	Non-parametric, instance-based supervised learning.
What does "instance-based" mean for k-NN?	No explicit training phase; memorizes entire training dataset.
How does k-NN classify a new data point?	Assigns majority label of its `k` closest neighbors.
What is the role of the hyperparameter `k` in k-NN?	Controls model complexity and decision boundary.
What is the effect of a small `k` (e.g., k=1) in k-NN?	Low bias, high variance (overfitting), sensitive to noise.
What is the effect of a large `k` in k-NN?	High bias, low variance (underfitting), smoother decision boundary.
How is the optimal `k` typically chosen for k-NN?	Using cross-validation to find the best score.
What is the most common distance metric used in k-NN?	Euclidean distance (L2 norm).
Why is feature scaling crucial for k-NN?	Sensitive to feature scale; large ranges dominate distance.
What is a common method for feature scaling before k-NN?	Standardization (zero mean, unit variance).
What is a disadvantage of k-NN regarding computational cost?	Computationally expensive at test time.
What is a disadvantage of k-NN regarding memory?	Requires large memory to store entire dataset.
Can k-NN learn complex decision boundaries?	Yes, it can learn complex, non-linear decision boundaries.
What is the primary purpose of Logistic Regression?	To model probabilities for classification tasks.
How does Logistic Regression model probabilities?	Passes linear model output through a sigmoid function.
What is the formula for the sigmoid function?	σ(z) = 1 / (1 + e⁻ᶻ)
What loss function is minimized in Logistic Regression?	Negative log-likelihood (cross-entropy loss).
What is the decision boundary in Logistic Regression?	A linear boundary where probability is 0.5 (wᵀx = 0).
What is the core principle of Support Vector Machines (SVMs)?	Maximize the margin between classes.
What is the "margin" in SVMs?	Distance between decision boundary and closest data points.
What are "support vectors" in SVMs?	Data points lying exactly on the margin, defining the boundary.
What is the "kernel trick" in SVMs used for?	To handle non-linearly separable data by mapping to higher dimensions.
Name two common kernel functions used in SVMs.	Polynomial kernel, Radial Basis Function (RBF) kernel.
How does Logistic Regression handle non-linearity?	Requires manual feature engineering.
How do SVMs handle non-linearity?	Easily with the kernel trick.
What type of output does Logistic Regression provide?	Probabilities.
What type of output does SVM typically provide?	Class labels.
What is the underlying principle of Logistic Regression?	Probabilistic (maximizes likelihood).
What is the underlying principle of SVMs?	Geometric (maximizes margin).
