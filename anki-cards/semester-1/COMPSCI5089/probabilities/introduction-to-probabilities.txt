What is an experiment or trial in probability?	An occurrence with an uncertain outcome
What is the sample space in probability?	The set of all possible outcomes for an experiment
What is the definition of probability for an event?	The ratio of outcomes in the event to the total outcomes in the sample space
What is the Bayesian (Laplacian) view of probability?	It treats probability as a calculus of belief or degree of confidence
What is the Frequentist view of probability?	It considers probability to be the long-term frequency of repeated events
What is the Boundedness axiom of probability?	For any event A, 0 <= P(A) <= 1
What is the Unitarity axiom of probability?	The sum of probabilities for all possible outcomes in a sample space is 1
What is the Sum Rule of probability for two events A and B?	P(A or B) = P(A) + P(B) - P(A and B)
What is conditional probability P(A|B)?	The probability of A happening given that B has already happened
What is a random variable?	A variable that can take on different values, but its specific value is unknown
What function describes the probabilities of a discrete random variable?	Probability Mass Function (PMF)
What function describes the probabilities of a continuous random variable?	Probability Density Function (PDF)
What must any PMF or PDF sum or integrate to over all possible outcomes?	1
What is the expectation or expected value E[X] of a random variable?	The "average" value, weighted by the probability of each outcome
What is the mean of a random variable?	Its expectation, E[X]
What is the variance of a random variable?	A measure of spread, E[(X-E[X])^2]
Why do we often use log-probabilities?	To avoid numerical underflow by summing logs instead of multiplying small probabilities
