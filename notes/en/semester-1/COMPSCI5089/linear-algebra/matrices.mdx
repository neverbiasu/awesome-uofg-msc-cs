## Overview
- **Goal**: To understand how matrices represent data and data transformations, and to learn the fundamental operations performed on them.
- **Scope**: This note covers the definition of matrices, basic arithmetic, transposition, and multiplication.

## Learning Objectives
1. Define a matrix and identify its dimensions.
2. Perform matrix addition, scalar multiplication, and transposition.
3. Understand the rules and process of matrix multiplication.
4. Recognize special matrices like the identity and inverse matrix.

## Key Knowledge Points

### 1. Matrix Fundamentals
- **Motivation**: Matrices are fundamental in data science for storing data (e.g., a dataset where rows are samples and columns are features) and for representing linear transformations that can scale, rotate, or shear data vectors.
- **Core Concepts**:
  - **Matrix**: A rectangular array of numbers arranged in rows and columns.
  - **Matrix Addition**: Element-wise sum of two matrices of the same dimensions.
  - **Scalar Multiplication**: Multiplying every element of a matrix by a scalar.
  - **Matrix Transposition**: Swapping the rows and columns of a matrix. The transpose of **A** is denoted **A**<sup>T</sup>.
  - **Matrix Multiplication**: A more complex operation where the product of two matrices **A** (m x n) and **B** (n x p) results in a new matrix **C** (m x p). The number of columns in the first matrix must equal the number of rows in the second.
  - **Identity Matrix**: A square matrix with ones on the main diagonal and zeros elsewhere. It is the multiplicative identity for matrices.
  - **Inverse Matrix**: For a square matrix **A**, its inverse **A**<sup>-1</sup> is a matrix such that **A** **A**<sup>-1</sup> = **I** (the identity matrix).
- **Notes/Best Practices**:
  - **Matrix multiplication is not commutative**: In general, **AB** ≠ **BA**.
  - Always check for dimension compatibility before attempting matrix multiplication.
- **Summary**: Matrices provide a powerful way to organize and manipulate data. Understanding their operations is key to working with linear models and data transformations.
