## Key Knowledge Points

### 1. What is Research?
- **Motivation:** To establish a clear, foundational understanding of what constitutes "research" in an academic and practical context, distinguishing it from simple information gathering.
- **Core Concepts:** Research is defined in several ways:
    - A systematic process of inquiry involving data collection, documentation, analysis, and interpretation. (Hampshire College)
    - A systematic investigation to establish facts and reach new conclusions. (OED)
    - The creation of new knowledge, or the use of existing knowledge in a new and creative way.
    - For this course, **research is about solving problems**, making it a form of design.
- **Use Cases/Patterns:** This concept applies to all academic work, especially the dissertation. It provides the guiding principle for any project that aims to discover or create a solution to a problem.
- **Example:**
  ```text
  # Conceptual Example
  # Investigating a new algorithm's performance on a novel dataset is research.
  # Simply using a well-known library to build a standard application is development, not research.
  ```
- **Notes/Best Practices:** Avoid the misconception that research is just collecting facts. The key is the *creation* of new insights or solutions.
- **Summary:** Research is a systematic, creative process of inquiry designed to solve problems and generate new knowledge.

### 2. Core Research Skills
- **Motivation:** To outline the practical, transferable skills needed to plan, execute, and communicate research effectively.
- **Core Concepts:** The essential skills for a researcher include:
  1.  Identifying a problem.
  2.  Understanding the context of the problem.
  3.  Finding out what is already known (literature search).
  4.  Coming up with a new solution.
  5.  Evaluating the solution.
  6.  Communicating the solution to others.
- **Use Cases/Patterns:** These skills are applied sequentially and iteratively throughout the dissertation process, from topic selection and proposal to writing the final report.
- **Example:**
  ```text
  # Dissertation Workflow Example
  # 1. Identify Problem: Existing recommendation systems give poor results for users with sparse data.
  # 2. Understand Context: Study how current collaborative filtering methods work.
  # 3. Literature Search: Find papers on "cold-start recommendation problem".
  # 4. New Solution: Propose a hybrid model using content-based features for new users.
  # 5. Evaluate: Test the model against a baseline on a public dataset.
  # 6. Communicate: Write the dissertation and present the findings.
  ```
- **Notes/Best Practices:** These skills are not a one-time checklist but form an iterative cycle. For example, evaluation findings might require you to refine your solution or even your understanding of the problem.
- **Summary:** Successful research depends on a continuous cycle of identifying, understanding, solving, evaluating, and communicating your work.

### 3. Course Structure and Assessment
- **Motivation:** To clarify the expectations, deliverables, and logistics of the COMPSCI5092 course, enabling students to plan their work effectively.
- **Core Concepts:** The course is built on three main themes: `Research methods`, `Professional skills`, and `Study skills`.
    - **Structure:** It is delivered through lectures and tutorials. Tutorials are key for discussion and feedback.
    - **Assessment:** The course is 100% assessed by a portfolio of work submitted at the end of the semester.
- **Use Cases/Patterns:** This information is critical for course planning and time management. The portfolio assignments are designed as stepping stones that build upon each other.
- **Example:**
  ```text
  # Portfolio Item Connection Example
  # The 'Literature Search' assignment directly feeds into the
  # 'Critical Review of a Research Paper', which then helps inform
  # the 'Research Proposal'.
  ```
- **Notes/Best Practices:** The portfolio is the only assessment; therefore, consistent effort on each piece of work is crucial. All official information, resources, and submission links are on the course's Moodle page.
- **Summary:** The course is assessed via a single portfolio of interconnected tasks designed to progressively build your research skills for the dissertation.

## Best Practices
- View research as a structured, systematic problem-solving process, not random exploration.
- Actively practice and develop each of the core research skills, as they are transferable to any complex project.
- Use the portfolio assignments as direct building blocks for your final dissertation proposal.
- Regularly consult the official Moodle page as the single source of truth for all course information, deadlines, and resources.
- Engage actively in tutorials to clarify concepts and get essential feedback on your work.
