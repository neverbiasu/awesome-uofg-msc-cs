## Key Takeaway
Sketching explores the design space, while prototyping explores the user experience over time. Low-fidelity methods like paper prototyping are essential for early, honest feedback.

## Sketching vs. Prototyping
Bill Buxton's distinction is crucial for understanding the design process.
-   **Sketching**: Explores the **composition in space**. It is about invention and generating many alternatives.
-   **Prototyping**: Explores the **idea in time**. It is about manifesting a specific design choice to test how it functions.

## Paper Prototyping
Paper prototyping is a low-fidelity method where users interact with a paper version of an interface.

### The "Playing Computer" Role
A member of the design team acts as the computer, moving paper elements in response to user actions **without explaining** how the interface works.
<Steps>

### Advantages of Paper Prototyping
1.  **Early Problem Identification**: Find usability issues before a single line of code is written.
2.  **Better Feedback**: Users are less "nitpicky" about aesthetics and focus on workflow.
3.  **Avoids Opinion Wars**: Data from interaction overrides personal preferences.
</Steps>

## Advanced Prototyping Methods

### Wizard of Oz (WoZ)
A technique where a human "wizard" simulates system responses that aren't yet implemented (e.g., an AI response being typed by a human behind the scenes). The user believes they are interacting with a fully functional system.

### Video Prototyping
A storyboard-like video that illustrates how a user would interact with a system in a specific context.
-   **Purpose**: Communicate the "solution" to stakeholders and evaluate the conceptual design.
-   **Key Component**: Storyboarding (similar to a comic book).

## Examiner's Perspective

### Hard Red Lines: Wizard of Oz Setup
In an exam, if asked how to test a complex AI system that hasn't been built yet, **Wizard of Oz** is the correct answer. You must mention that the user is "fooled" to ensure authentic behavior.

### Technical Nuance: Fidelity
-   **Low-fidelity (Lo-fi)**: Fast, cheap, focus on structure.
-   **High-fidelity (Hi-fi)**: Realistic, focus on detail and performance.
-   *Tip*: Do not jump to Hi-fi too early; it makes designers "attached" to their first idea.

### Failure Analysis: "Playing Computer"
If the "computer" person starts explaining the interface to the user, the test is **invalid**. The system must speak for itself through the interaction.
