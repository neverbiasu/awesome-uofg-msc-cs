## Key Takeaway
Effective design starts with a deep understanding of the "Wicked Problem" and proceeds through a structured funnel of divergent ideation and convergent critique.

## Defining the Problem
HCI problems are often **Wicked Problems**: they lack clarity in both aims and solutions, involve changing human behavior, and are socially complex.

### The Problem Statement Template
To define a problem clearly, you must answer three questions:
1.  **Who?**: For whom is the situation consequential?
2.  **Why?**: What causes the consequence?
3.  **So what?**: What is the negative consequence?

## Ideation & Thinking Modes
Ideation involves two distinct modes of thinking that **must be kept separate**.

### Two Modes of Thinking
-   **Idea Creation (Divergent)**: Generating as many ideas as possible for exploration. Quantity leads to quality.
-   **Critiquing (Convergent)**: Reviewing and judging ideas to filter for the best ones.
![Two Modes of Thinking in Ideation](/images/COMPSCI5057/week8/week_8__creative_design-021.png)
<Callout type="warn">
**The Golden Rule**: Do not interrupt idea creation with critiques. Killing an idea too early prevents further exploration.
</Callout>

## The Design Funnel
Design is a cycle of **Elaboration** (generating options) and **Reduction** (narrowing down).
-   **Elaboration**: Creating a wide variety of design concepts.
-   **Reduction**: Using critiques and evaluation to select the most promising concept.
![Design Funnel](/images/COMPSCI5057/week8/week_8__creative_design-034.png)

## Examiner's Perspective

### Hard Red Lines: Idea Origin
Design ideas **do not come from users**. It is the designer's job to discover latent needs that users cannot articulate.
- *Exam Tip*: If a question asks if you should just "ask users what they want", the answer is usually **No**; you should observe and synthesize.

### Technical Nuance: Effective Brainstorming
-   **Lateral Thinking**: Thinking outside the box.
-   **No "War Stories"**: Keep the focus on generating new ideas, not recounting old experiences.
-   **Build on others**: Use "Yes, and..." rather than "No, but...".
