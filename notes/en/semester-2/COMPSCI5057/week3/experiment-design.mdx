## Key Takeaway
A robust HCI experiment requires carefully defined Independent and Dependent variables, and must account for individual differences or order effects through Between-Subjects or Within-Subjects designs.
# Experimental Design
![Variables in HCI Experiments](/images/COMPSCI5057/week1/week_1__introduction-009.png)

## Variables
In any experiment, we manipulate and measure specific factors:
- **Independent Variable (IV)**: The factor that the researcher manipulates (e.g., Input Method: Mouse vs. Trackpad).
- **Dependent Variable (DV)**: The outcome that is measured (e.g., Task Completion Time, Error Rate).
- **Control Variable**: Factors that are kept constant to prevent interference (e.g., Screen size, Lighting conditions).

## Experimental Designs
We must decide how to assign participants to different conditions.

### Within-Subjects Design (Repeated Measures)
Each participant performs **all** experimental conditions.
- **Pros**: Requires fewer participants; eliminates individual differences (each person acts as their own control).
- **Cons**: Susceptible to **Learning Effects** (getting better over time) and **Fatigue Effects**.
- **Mitigation**: Use **Counterbalancing** (e.g., half do A then B, half do B then A) to cancel out order effects.

### Between-Subjects Design (Independent Measures)
Each participant performs **only one** experimental condition.
- **Pros**: No learning or fatigue effects across conditions.
- **Cons**: Requires more participants; individual differences (e.g., age, skill) can skew results.
- **Mitigation**: Random assignment of participants to groups.

## Hypothesis Testing
Experiments are designed to test hypotheses.
- **Null Hypothesis ($H_0$)**: Assumes there is **no difference** between conditions (e.g., "There is no difference in speed between Layout A and Layout B").
- **Alternative Hypothesis ($H_1$)**: Assumes there **is a difference** (e.g., "Layout A is faster than Layout B").
Our goal is to gather enough evidence to **reject the null hypothesis** with a certain level of confidence (typically $p < 0.05$).

## Key Takeaway
- **Control is Key**: Always identify and control confounding variables.
- **Counterbalancing**: Essential for Within-Subjects designs to neutralize order effects.
- **Validity**: Internal validity (did the IV cause the change in DV?) vs. External validity (do results generalize to the real world?).
