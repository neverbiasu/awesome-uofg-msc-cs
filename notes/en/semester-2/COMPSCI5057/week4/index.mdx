## Summary

This week focuses on quantitative methods for evaluating human-computer interfaces. It contrasts analytic methods (expert inspection) with empirical methods (data from real users) and introduces the statistical tools necessary to prove that a design improvement is not due to chance.

<Accordions>
  <Accordion title="Evaluation Categories">
    Distinguishing between **Analytic** (attributes of the design, e.g., heuristic evaluation) and **Empirical** (observed user data, e.g., lab studies) methods.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Usability Constructs">
    Defining usability through ISO 9241-11: Effectiveness, Efficiency, and Satisfaction. Understanding User Experience (UX) as a broader concept encompassing utility and hedonic quality.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Measurement Scales">
    Classification of data: Nominal (categories), Ordinal (ranking), Interval (equal distance, no absolute zero), and Ratio (absolute zero).
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Hypothesis Testing">
    Using statistical tests (t-test, ANOVA) to reject the Null Hypothesis ($H_0$). Understanding $p$-values, effect sizes, and Type I/II errors.
  </Accordion>
</Accordions>
