## Key Takeaway
Qualitative methods like ethnography and semi-structured interviews reveal the "why" behind user behavior, uncovering latent needs and social contexts that quantitative data might miss.
# Qualitative Methods
![Qualitative Research Process](/images/COMPSCI5057/week9/HCIDE2026_week_9_revisit-010.png)

## Overview
- Goal: Apply qualitative research methods to understand user practices and social contexts to inform better design.
- Scope: Ethnography in HCI, Fieldwork (Observation/Interviews), Qualitative Analysis (Grounded Theory and Coding).

## Learning Objectives
1. Explain how ethnography moves beyond simple interaction to understand "practice."
2. Apply ethical principles to user research design.
3. Compare structured vs. semi-structured interview protocols.
4. Execute the three stages of qualitative coding.

## Key Concepts

### Ethnography in HCI
- Motivation: Traditional surveys and interviews can miss what users *actually* do. Ethnography focuses on real-world practices in their natural context.
- Definition: Literally "writing about a people or culture."
- Perspectives:
    - **Situated Actions**: Understanding actions within their specific time, space, and social dynamics.
    - **Insider's Perspective**: Describing scenes from the viewpoint of the participants.
    - **Turn to Practice**: Viewing technology as just one aspect of broader social practices.

### Fieldwork: Observation & Interviews
- Observation Roles:
    - **Complete Observer**: Passive watching without participation.
    - **Participant Observation**: Balancing involvement in the community with professional distance.
    - **Complete Participant**: Fully immersion, risking "going native" (losing detachment).
- Interviews:
    - **Structured**: Same questions for everyone (like a verbal questionnaire).
    - **Semi-structured**: Use an interview protocol with open-ended questions and flexibility for follow-ups.
    - **Unstructured**: Open-ended conversation for early exploration.
- **Tricks for Concrete Info**: Ask about specific past events ("the last time you...") rather than general habits ("usually").

### Qualitative Data Analysis (Coding)
- Motivation: To derive meaningful themes from raw transcripts or field notes.
- **Stages of Coding (Grounded Theory)**:
    - **Open Coding**: Identifying distinct pieces of information and assigning short phrases (codes).
    - **Axial Coding**: Organizing open codes into categories and identifying relationships between them.
    - **Selective Coding**: Combining categories into an overarching central theme.
- **In-vivo Coding**: Using the participants' own words to define codes.

### Ethics in User Research
- Core Principles:
    - Informed Consent (verbal or written).
    - Right to withdraw at any time.
    - Anonymity and data security.
    - Minimizing risk and ensuring participants are not in a position of authority under the researcher.

## Best Practices
- **Neutral Phrasing**: Avoid leading questions (e.g., "How did you feel?" instead of "Were you unhappy?").
- **Triangulation**: Cross-check info from "star users" (key informants) with others to ensure reliability.
- **Immersive Notes**: Record interactional details and indigenous meanings contemporaneously.
