## Key Takeaway
CNNs exploit the spatial local correlation of images through shared weights and pooling, drastically reducing the number of parameters compared to fully connected networks while achieving translation invariance.

## Why CNN for Images?
Fully connected networks (MLPs) fail on images because:
1.  **Parameter Explosion**: A $1000 \times 1000$ image would require millions of weights per neuron.
2.  **Loss of Spatial Structure**: MLPs flatten images into 1D vectors, losing the relative positions of pixels.

## Core Components of CNN

### Convolutional Layer
-   **Kernels/Filters**: Small matrices that slide across the image to detect features (edges, textures).
-   **Stride**: The step size of the filter.
-   **Padding**: Adding zeros around the borders to control output size.

### Pooling Layer
-   Reduces spatial dimensions (downsampling).
-   **Max Pooling**: Keeps the most prominent feature in a window.
-   **Advantage**: Provides **Invariance** to small translations and distortions.

## Calculation Formulas
-   **Output Width ($W_{out}$)**: $\frac{W_{in} - F + 2P}{S} + 1$
-   *Where*: $W$ = Width, $F$ = Filter size, $P$ = Padding, $S$ = Stride.

## Examiner's Perspective: Technical Nuances

### Receptive Field
-   **Concept**: The specific area of the input image that a neuron in a deep layer "sees".
-   **Nuance**: Deeper layers have larger receptive fields, allowing them to detect complex, global features (like faces) rather than just local edges.

### Translation Invariance
-   **Hard Red Line**: CNNs are invariant to *small* translations due to pooling, but not necessarily to rotation or scaling. Data augmentation is usually needed to handle those.
