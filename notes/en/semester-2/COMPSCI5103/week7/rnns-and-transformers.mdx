## Key Takeaway
Transformers have revolutionized sequence modeling by replacing the sequential constraints of RNNs with a parallelizable self-attention mechanism, enabling models to capture long-range dependencies efficiently.

## 1. Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs)
RNNs process data sequentially.
-   **Hidden State**: Serves as the memory of the network.
-   **Challenge**: Vanishing/Exploding gradients in long sequences.
-   **Solution**: **LSTMs** and **GRUs** introduce gating mechanisms to control memory flow.

## 2. The Self-Attention Mechanism
The core innovation of the Transformer.
-   **Parallelization**: All tokens in a sequence are processed simultaneously.
-   **Context**: Each token "attends" to all other tokens to calculate its representation.
-   **Formulas**: $Attention(Q, K, V) = softmax(\frac{QK^T}{\sqrt{d_k}})V$.

## 3. Transformer Components
-   **Multi-Head Attention**: Captures different semantic relationships.
-   **Positional Encoding**: Adds order information back into the parallel model.
-   **Layer Norm & Residuals**: Ensures stable training of deep stacks.

## 4. Examiner's Perspective: Technical Nuances

### Why Transformers beat RNNs?
-   **RNN**: $O(N)$ depth for dependencies. Hard to parallelize.
-   **Transformer**: $O(1)$ depth for dependencies (direct attention). Highly parallelizable.
