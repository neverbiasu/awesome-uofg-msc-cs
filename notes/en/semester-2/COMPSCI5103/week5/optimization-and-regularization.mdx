## Key Takeaway
Effective deep learning requires a balance between optimization (minimizing training loss) and regularization (preventing overfitting) to ensure the model generalizes well to new data.

## 1. Advanced Optimizers
Standard SGD is often too slow or gets stuck in local minima.
-   **Momentum**: Adds a fraction of the previous update to the current one to accelerate in the right direction.
-   **RMSProp**: Adapts the learning rate for each parameter based on recent magnitudes of gradients.
-   **Adam (Adaptive Moment Estimation)**: Combines Momentum and RMSProp. The current industry standard for most tasks.

## 2. Generalization and Overfitting
-   **Overfitting**: Model performs well on training data but poorly on validation data (high variance).
-   **Underfitting**: Model fails to learn the training data (high bias).

## 3. Regularization Techniques
Methods to prevent overfitting by penalizing model complexity.
-   **L2 Regularization (Weight Decay)**: Adds $\lambda \sum w^2$ to the loss function.
-   **Dropout**: Randomly "drops out" neurons during training to prevent co-adaptation.
-   **Early Stopping**: Stop training when validation loss starts to increase.
<Callout type="info">
**Batch Normalization**: Normalizes the inputs of each layer to reduce internal covariate shift, allowing for higher learning rates and acting as a form of regularization.
</Callout>

## 4. Examiner's Perspective: Technical Nuances

### Bias-Variance Trade-off
-   **Hard Red Line**: You cannot simultaneously minimize both bias and variance. Regularization typically increases bias slightly but significantly reduces variance.

### The Role of Momentum
-   **Nuance**: Momentum helps "dampen" oscillations in steep directions while accelerating progress along the flat bottom of a ravine.
